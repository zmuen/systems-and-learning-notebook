# Degree distribution and the "friendship paradox"
## Degree distribution
Meaning: the fraction of nodes with degree k
- First moment: average degree
- Second moment: the average of squared degrees
- variance
### Complementary cumulative distribution
For larger networks, we usually do not show the empirical probability density function $p_{k}$.  Instead, we show the probability that the degree is at least k, for any k>=0.  

This is referred to as the Complementary Cumulative Distribution Function (denoted as C-CDF).  Note that $\overline{P}_{k}$ = Prob[degree>=k] is the sum of all $p_{x}$ values for x>=k. 

## Friendship paradox
The average neighbor degree is larger than the average node degree whenever the degree variance is positive.

Reason: When you sample through edges, highly connected nodes get counted repeatedly

## Degree correlations
One way to quantify the degree correlations in a network is by approximating the relationship between the avg. nearest neighbor degree ${k}_{nn}(k)$ and the degree k with a power-law of the form:

${k}_{nn}(k) \approx a \cdot {k}^{\mu}$ 

- if $\mu$ > 0: higher-degree nodes tend to have higher-degree neighbors, the network is assortative
- if $\mu$ > 0: higher-degree nodes tend to have lower-degree neighbors, the network is disassortative
- if $\mu$ is statistically not significantly different from zero, we say that the network is neutral.
