# Small-world property
a mathematical characteristic of certain networks where most nodes are not direct neighbors, but any node can be reached from any other node through a very small number of steps or edges. This reflects the "six degrees of separation" concept seen in social systems.

Core characteristics:
- Short Average Path Length: The typical distance between two random nodes grows slowly—proportionally to the logarithm of the total number of nodes.
- High Clustering Coefficient: Neighbors of a specific node are heavily interconnected, forming tight local cliques or neighborhoods (transitivity).
- Sparseness: The total number of edges is much lower than the maximum possible number of connections between vertices.

Therefore, the notion of small-world networks depends on two concepts:
